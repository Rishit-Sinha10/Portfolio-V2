## Overall take
Your portfolio already has a coherent **modern, minimalist editorial** direction: a narrow content column, black-and-white palette, serif nameplate, sans-serif body, monospaced section labels, and restrained borders. The live page feels personal rather than like a generic template.
The main weakness is not a lack of visual elements—it’s that the portfolio currently behaves more like a **personal status page** than a portfolio that quickly proves your value to a recruiter or client. I would prioritize fixing correctness, clarifying your professional positioning, and adding evidence of project impact before adding more widgets or animation.
## What’s working
- **Consistent visual language.** The serif/sans/mono type pairing and light/dark tokens are a solid foundation in `globals.css`.
- **Useful content breadth.** The homepage covers experience, projects, writing, skills, highlights, and GitHub activity in `Home.tsx`.
- **Good baseline accessibility.** The site has a skip link, focus styles, labeled navigation, and accessible accordion controls in `layout.tsx` and `accordion-card.tsx`.
- **Solid SEO foundation.** Page metadata, Open Graph data, and Person structured data are set up in `layout.tsx`.
- **Project descriptions explain the technical work.** The shared project data in `projects.ts` is a good starting point for stronger case studies.
## Highest-priority issues
1. **The live homepage reports a React hydration error.** I saw React error `#418` on repeated page loads of [the deployed site](https://www.rishitsinha.online/). The most likely cause is rendering the current time during server rendering and again during client hydration: `Home.tsx`. The interval state update also triggers a render every second, although the `time` state value itself is unused at `Home.tsx`. Make the initial output deterministic, then start the clock after mount—or remove the live clock.
2. **Your career information disagrees across pages.** The homepage lists Harnav Ventures as current and dates the other roles in `Home.tsx`. The separate experience route lists different roles and dates in `experience page`. The About copy also describes you as a Shopify Developer in `about-us.tsx`. Confirm the accurate timeline and use it consistently.
3. **The “Now” page is already stale.** It says “Last updated September 2026” at `now/page.tsx`, while the current date is November 2026. It also duplicates project names and status data; for example, it uses “klyro” while the shared project data uses “klyro.” Update it and avoid maintaining project status in multiple places.
4. **The first screen needs a clearer professional proposition.** The name is prominent, but the next details are age, a seconds-updating clock, music, and social icons; the role and strongest reason to hire you are less prominent. The “Book a call” and resume links come later in `Home.tsx`. Replace some of that space with a direct headline—your role, specialty, and what you’re open to—and keep one or two primary actions visible immediately.
5. **Projects need proof, not just descriptions.** Your summaries explain what each project does, but don’t show your specific contribution, user or business impact, technical trade-offs, or a visual of the finished product. The detail route in `work/[slug]/page.tsx` currently repeats the summary, and project cards don’t link to it. Build two or three substantial case studies first: screenshots, your role, key decisions, and truthful measurable outcomes. Link to them from the project list.
## What I’d add—and what I’d trim
- **Add a sharp role statement and availability** near your name. Make it specific to the work you want, rather than relying on “CS student” alone.
- **Add two or three visual case studies** for your strongest projects. A clear before/after or architecture visual can do more than extra interface effects.
- **Add verified proof** for the Sentry OSS selection, project outcomes, or work experience—link to a source where possible. The highlights in `Highlights.tsx` currently look clickable because of their arrow treatment, but they aren’t links.
- **Show recent writing on the homepage.** The current writing section is a generic introduction plus a link in `Home.tsx`; two recent posts with dates would make that section more useful.
Keep the monochrome system. If you want one signature accent, the existing blue project color could be used selectively for links, status, or a call to action; avoid adding decoration just to make the page feel more “designed.” Your grayscale illustrated avatar gives it personality; keep it if intentional, or use a professional photo if recruiter trust is the priority.
## Code-quality notes
- **Lint currently fails:** `npm run lint` reports **14 errors and 23 warnings**. Examples include ref access during render in `github.tsx`, mount-effect state updates in `link_preview.tsx` and `navbar.tsx`, plus explicit `any` types in `api/spotify/route.ts`. There are also unused imports and state.
- The homepage component has grown to contain substantial content data and UI logic in one file. Split it selectively as you add case studies, and make the experience/project data single-source so the pages don’t drift.
- The root layout already provides a `<main>`, but the case-study route renders another `<main>` in `work/[slug]/page.tsx`. Use a non-main wrapper there.
- Several imports in `app/page.tsx` are unused; that route only renders the portfolio.
**Recommended order:**  →  refine mobile social links and text sizing. I reviewed the routes and components, but didn’t make changes.
